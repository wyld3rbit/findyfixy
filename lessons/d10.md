# Day 10. Capstone and show and tell

**Outcome:** one command starts the whole stack, a 3-minute live demo, 6 slides, and a one-page architecture decision record.

## Concept (15 min)

**Reproducibility is the first credibility test.** A demo that only runs on the author's machine proves the author's machine. `make setup` and `make demo` from a fresh clone prove the system: pinned dependencies, pinned images, no hidden state, no absolute paths. Reviewers and interviewers assume the worst about anything they cannot run.

**An architecture decision record captures why, not what.** Code shows what was built. It cannot show the alternatives you rejected, the constraints that forced the choice or the conditions under which you would reverse it. An ADR records those in one page, so the decision can be challenged on its reasoning later instead of being relitigated from scratch.

**Larson's strategy structure** maps cleanly onto an ADR.

- **Diagnosis** states the problem with evidence. If the diagnosis is wrong, nothing after it matters.
- **Guiding policies** are the few rules that make later decisions easy: how resources are allocated, which rules are non-negotiable, who decides what.
- **Coherent actions** are the mechanisms that enforce the policies: gates, budgets, scopes, and the path for exceptions.

A policy with no enforcing action is a wish. An action with no policy behind it is busywork.

**A credible demo shows failure handled, not only success.** Routine questions answered cheaply is table stakes. The escalation, the approval pause and the blocked injection are what show the system was engineered.

## Part 1. One-command demo (40 min)

Add a `Makefile` with three targets:

- `make setup`: checks that uv, Docker Compose and Ollama are present, pulls both models, runs `uv sync`.
- `make demo`: checks the container runtime is up, starts the app stack and the Langfuse stack, runs ingestion, opens the demo screen and Langfuse in the browser.
- `make stop`: brings everything down.

Then `scripts/demo_incidents.sh`, which fires 5 incidents in order:

1. Routine: answered by the small model.
2. Routine with a near-duplicate in memory: shows recall.
3. Hard: escalates to the large model.
4. Needs rollback: pauses for approval.
5. Poisoned retrieval: policy blocks the injected action.

Test it from a fresh clone in a new folder. If it does not run first time, fix it before anything else today.

Write `README.md`: what it does, an architecture diagram, `make setup` and `make demo`, the numbers, and the open-source bill of materials from `00-plan.md`.

## Part 2. Show and tell kit (40 min)

`docs/show-and-tell.md`:

**3-minute script.**

- 0:00 The problem, in one number: senior engineer hours lost to triage.
- 0:20 The design on one diagram: router, memory, policy, broker, traces.
- 0:50 Live: incidents 1 to 3 on the demo screen. Point at the model used and the recalled incidents.
- 1:40 Live: incident 4. Click Approve. Incident 5: show the block.
- 2:20 Langfuse: one trace tree, cost per run, escalation rate.
- 2:40 The numbers: recall@5, faithfulness, cost before and after, attacks blocked. What you would do next.

**6 slides.**

1. Problem and the number.
2. Architecture diagram.
3. Three decisions and their trade-offs: small-model-first, direct APIs over MCP on hot paths, model proposes and policy decides.
4. Results table from your notes.
5. Open-source stack, one line each.
6. What breaks at 100x, and what you would build next.

Record the demo once with QuickTime as a backup in case the live run fails.

## Part 3. ADR and drills (40 min)

`docs/adr-001-triage-agent.md`, in Larson's strategy format, one page:

- **Diagnosis.** The problem in numbers: triage load, time to resolution, cost of senior attention.
- **Guiding policies.**
    - Resource allocation: small-model-first, larger model on measured escalation only.
    - Fundamental rules: the model proposes and policy decides; no long-lived secrets in the agent; no merge below the quality gate.
    - Decision-making: who can change thresholds, prompts and models, and how.
- **Coherent actions.**
    - Enforcement: CI gates, gateway budgets, broker scopes.
    - Escalation: the human-approval tier and the on-call path.
    - Transitions: shadow, canary, rollout, rollback.
- **Measured results.** Recall@5, faithfulness, cost per run, escalation rate, attacks blocked.

Then answer each aloud in under 90 seconds:

1. "Walk me through an LLM system you built end to end."
2. "What breaks first at 100x traffic?" (gateway rate limits, rerank latency, judge cost, index size)
3. "How do you organise teams around an AI platform?" (durable teams, individual-owned migrations, the harness as shared infrastructure)
4. "How would you define maturity levels for AI-assisted engineering, and what does each level require?" Connect each level to a harness control you built this sprint.
5. "What would you not let an agent do, and why?"
